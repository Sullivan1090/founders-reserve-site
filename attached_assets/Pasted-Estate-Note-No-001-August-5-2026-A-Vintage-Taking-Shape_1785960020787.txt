Estate Note No. 001

August 5, 2026

A Vintage Taking Shape

One of the things I've learned over the years is that a vintage is never defined by a single event. It's the accumulation of hundreds of small moments, decisions, and conditions that begin long before the first grape is picked.

As we head toward harvest, there's a real sense of optimism around Napa Valley. Veraison is well underway, the vines are beginning to shift their energy toward ripening, and vineyards across the valley are entering the final stretch of the growing season. This is the point where patience becomes one of the most important tools we have.

Here at Sullivan Rutherford Estate, one of the defining characteristics of the 2026 vintage has been a naturally lighter crop. That story really began months ago.

During bloom, we experienced several rain events that interrupted flowering and pollination. Not every flower successfully became a berry, so fruit set was lighter than normal across our vineyard portfolio. All three of our vineyard sites came in lighter than we typically expect, with Crystal Springs and Soda Canyon seeing some of the most significant reductions.

While no grower hopes for fewer clusters, nature sometimes has a way of finding balance.

With fewer clusters to support, the vines are able to concentrate more of their energy into the fruit that's left. Smaller crops often translate into smaller berries, thicker skins, and greater concentration, giving us fruit with exceptional depth, structure, and intensity while maintaining freshness and balance.

The warm weather we've experienced through the summer has continued to move the vintage forward. Combined with the lighter crop load, ripening has been progressing a little faster than average. Quite simply, when a vine has less fruit to ripen, it can devote more of its resources to each cluster. That has created beautiful, even development across the vineyard.

From here, everything comes down to timing.

The next few weeks are when flavor, tannin, acidity, and texture all begin to come together. Harvest isn't about chasing sugar. It's about waiting until every piece of the puzzle aligns. Those decisions often come down to a matter of days, sometimes even hours, and they ultimately define the character of every wine we produce.

Back at the winery, harvest preparations are already in full swing. Tanks have been cleaned and prepared, equipment has been serviced, barrels are ready, and the cellar is beginning its annual transformation. While harvest gets most of the attention, a successful vintage is built on months of preparation before the first bin of fruit ever reaches the crush pad.

Although there's still plenty of growing season ahead of us, the vineyard is balanced, the fruit looks incredibly clean, and the combination of lighter yields, healthy vines, and steady ripening has us incredibly excited about the wines taking shape.

Over the coming weeks, these Estate Notes will follow the vintage from the first picks through fermentation, barrel aging, and eventually into bottle. It's one of the most rewarding times of the year, and we're looking forward to sharing the journey with you.
